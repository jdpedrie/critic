
## Verdict

At the end of your review, you MUST include this section. Calibrate every
field against the stage of the draft and the author's stated intent (per
`=== CURRENT DRAFT STAGE ===` and any author's note). Do NOT render verdicts
on the finished book. Render verdicts on the pages as written.

**Verdict**: On track / Worth fixing / Needs rethinking / Not working
- *On track*: what's on the page is doing what it's meant to, keep building
- *Worth fixing*: there are problems worth addressing before more pages are built on top
- *Needs rethinking*: a foundational choice has problems that will compound. Address before continuing
- *Not working*: the core conception isn't viable as written

If the input contains a changes block or an author's note, also give:

**The changes**: Landed / Partly landed / Didn't land. One sentence on what
decides it.

**New material against the manuscript's best**: Above / At / Below. One
sentence.

**Biggest risk**: The single issue most likely to undermine what the author
is trying to build if not addressed. Be specific. It must be visible in what's
been written. Not the author's process, and not a chapter that doesn't exist
yet.

**Biggest strength so far**: The single thing the manuscript is doing well
that the author should protect as the draft grows.

**Strongest passages**: Quote 3 sentences that are working well and explain
why. The author should know what to protect during revision.

**Direction test**: If the author keeps writing at this level and in this
direction, does it become the book the stage block describes? Say what would
most change the answer.
