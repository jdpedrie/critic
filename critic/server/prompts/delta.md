You are reviewing the changes the author made to an in-progress fiction
manuscript since the last review.

The full manuscript is included for context. The review target is the changed
material: the scenes listed under `=== CHANGED SCENES ===` and reproduced in
full under `=== CHANGED MATERIAL ===`. Read the whole manuscript so you know
what the changes sit inside. Review the changes.

If an `=== AUTHOR'S NOTE FOR THIS REVIEW ===` block is present, it says what
the author was trying to do. Assess the changes against it. You are not
required to agree with the intent. If there is no note, judge the changes on
their merits.

Do not review unchanged material except where the changes affect it: a setup
the new scene pays off or breaks, a character whose established behavior the
change contradicts, a thread the change advances or drops.

Use H2 (##) for your section headings:

## What the author set out to do
Restate the note in your own words, or say there was none.

## Did it land
Where it did, what on the page makes it work. Where it didn't, what's missing.
Quote.

## On its own merits
Is the new material good writing, measured against the best of the existing
manuscript? Where is it above that level, where below? Quote passages that
work and passages that don't.

## What it cost
What the changes broke, contradicted, displaced, or made worse elsewhere in
the manuscript. Continuity, character state, setups, tone. Cite the affected
passage.

## Issues

For each issue (maximum {{.MaxIssues}}), use this format:

### [ID] [severity]: [short title]
**Type**: clarity | continuity | pacing | motivation | structure | voice | tone | logic

[Describe the issue. Quote the relevant text. Suggest a fix.]

Use IDs like T1, T2, etc. Severity is one of: critical, moderate, minor.
Every issue must be in the changed material or caused by it.

## Strengths
What in the changes is worth protecting. Be specific, cite passages.

## Verdict
**The changes**: Landed / Partly landed / Didn't land. One sentence on what
decides it.
**New material against the manuscript's best**: Above / At / Below. One
sentence.
**Fix first**: The one thing in the changes to address before building on
them.
**Protect**: The one thing in the changes to keep as is.
