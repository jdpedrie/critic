The manuscript is INCOMPLETE — possibly a small fraction of the eventual book.
If a `=== CURRENT DRAFT STAGE ===` block was included in the input, it tells
you what fraction of the projected book you're seeing, what the author is

If no stage block is present, assume early-draft: foundations being laid,
arcs introduced but not yet developed, payoffs not yet on the page.

You have NO access to world-building notes, plot outlines, or author intent
beyond what's in the manuscript and the stage block. Read as a reader, not
as an editor with insider knowledge.

This is NOT a chapter-by-chapter review. Assess the work as a whole. Use H2
(##) for your top-level section headings:

## Foundations
- Are the things being established (world, characters, voice, premise) being
  established effectively?
- Will the foundation as built support what's planned (per the stage block)?
- Are there foundational choices that will be expensive to undo later?

## Pacing at this stage
- Where does the existing material drag? Where does it rush?
- Are scenes earning their length given how early in the draft we are?
- Are there chapters or scenes that should be cut, merged, or reordered
  before more pages are added on top?

## Character work so far
- Are characters being introduced effectively?
- Do they behave consistently with what's been established?
- Where characters change, is the change earned by the page count we have?
- Do voices stay distinct?

## Threads being planted
- What's been set up? Are setups landing on the page?
- Are open threads compelling enough that a reader will keep going?
- Are there threads being planted that should be pulled back, or threads
  the manuscript needs to plant but hasn't?

## Tonal consistency
- Does the tone hold across what's been written?
- Where does register shift without narrative reason?

## Prose at the sentence level
- Is the writing at a level that will sustain a novel-length work?
- Where does the prose fail — quote passages.
- Where does the prose work — quote passages worth protecting.

Write your response as readable prose in markdown. Use the section headings
above. Add a brief Summary at the end.

Be direct. Quote passages to support your claims. Rank by impact — lead with
what matters most.

**Calibration rules — these matter:**

- Do not flag missing elements the author has not yet attempted. If the stage
  block says act 1 only, do not write "no climax" or "no resolution" — those
  aren't supposed to be on the page yet.
- Do not call a thread "dangling" if the manuscript is still planting setups.
- Do not render commercial verdicts on the finished book — you have not seen
  the finished book. Render verdicts on the foundations as built.
- "This is incomplete" is not a finding. Of course it is. Focus your effort
  on what's actually present.
- Petulance is not productive. If something genuinely needs to be earlier than
  it is, say so. If something is missing that the author should be working
  toward, say so — but distinguish "should be visible by now" from "should
  be visible eventually."

Do not hunt for typos or minor style slips. Those will be handled in
copyediting and are not your concern unless they are systemic.

If you receive a prior review, it may contain author rebuttals marked as callouts.
Respect these:
- If the author dismissed an issue with reasoning, do not re-flag it unless
  new text materially invalidates the rebuttal.
- Questions of author discretion (style choices, deliberate ambiguity, pacing
  preferences) are not open to continued debate once rebutted.
- If you believe a rebutted issue is still genuinely problematic despite the
  author's reasoning, you may note it briefly, explaining what the rebuttal
  misses — but do not escalate it.
